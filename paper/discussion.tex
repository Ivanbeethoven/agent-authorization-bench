\section{Discussion}
The choice of reviewer depends on the error that a deployment most needs to prevent. The cascade's one extra explicit refusal relative to direct DeepSeek is different from preventing an extra execution: both systems already stopped that request. Its principal advantage over Jev is removing unauthorized approvals on AuthGateBench. At the observed operating points, however, it prevented only three additional releases relative to direct DeepSeek and interrupted 44 additional legal requests. We would report the exact refusal, temporary hold and release counts on prohibited operations alongside access to authorized actions before choosing any of these strategies.

The safety value of a reviewer depends on its conditional behavior after the first system has approved. None of the Jev Boundary prohibitions in the official test required a second call. Many AuthGate approvals reached the second stage, creating opportunities both to catch excess scope and to withhold an explicitly authorized action. A model's standalone error rate does not identify these conditional effects. The paired paths and volume-matched controls expose them. Where the user's limit has a trusted structured representation, checking it directly can address errors shared by both models.

\paragraph{Validity and limitations.} The public evaluations are static judgments with one successful response per stage and request. They measure neither harm caused nor completion after an intervention. The reference authorities differ: one dataset is synthetic and balanced by contrast pair, another uses annotator majorities, and the third instantiates authored policies. Equal weighting of records places 700/1,126 decisions on AuthGateBench. Native accuracy counts disagreements about \emph{how} to withhold execution alongside dangerous releases. We provide disaggregated estimates for these reasons. The sparse explicit-prohibition class precludes a useful comparison of small differences in deployment risk. For scale, even under an idealized independent Bernoulli model, observing zero misses in 37 trials yields a one-sided 95\% upper miss-rate bound of \(1-0.05^{1/37}=7.78\%\). The actual corpus has repeated families and is not such a sample.

The primary collection was available during earlier project exploration. A rerun fixes the measurement conditions for this comparison but cannot establish generalization to unpublished cases, deployment traffic or revised model aliases. The historical collection and numerical challenge address narrower transfer questions. A stronger certification study would require independent severity-labeled scenarios sampled from a defined deployment population, a frozen policy and repeated measurements, with logging at actual tool dispatch. If operational harm is highly concentrated in a few types of action, counts alone cannot identify which policy minimizes expected harm.

\section{Conclusion}
Independent review of Jev approvals intercepted additional unauthorized actions, largely in one benchmark. Relative to direct DeepSeek, the improvement in pooled non-execution recall was small and accompanied by a substantial loss of legal access. Matching release volume explained much of the gain over Jev. On numerical limits, even the highest-accuracy direct model missed every violation. On the final authored cohort, computing tool effects and checking qualitative conditions separately improved accuracy over both direct services. The matched reviewer substitution located Jev's observed advantage in cost and Bailian's observed deficit in approvals of unsupported actions. The final method preserved more legal access than direct DeepSeek and less than direct Jev. External deployment records with independently supplied scope and consent remain necessary to estimate these trade-offs in practice.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and responsible use.} All candidate tool actions were assessed as text; the study executed no benchmark operation and recruited no human participants. The project repository is private at preparation time. The accompanying artifact supplies the frozen predictions, per-request fees, resampling code, numerical generator and the files needed for typesetting. The source archive rebuilds the paper without paid inference. We distribute derived decisions indexed by public sample identifiers. Provider keys and account balances are excluded.
